# Attended screen sharing

An assigned technician opens an accepted remote IT Service Request from the employee ticket or the Remote support workspace. The technician needs a current direct login with verified MFA. Requesting screen sharing places an approval request in the customer's Dashboard. The customer signs in directly, approves view-only access, and chooses a screen or window through the operating system and browser picker. The technician checks approval and connects from the same login used to request the session. Both participants can end it. The customer can also end sharing from the browser's capture indicator.

Batch B supplies the IT Service Request, technician assignment, accepted appointment state, and audit events. Its dispatch validates the reservation and payment receipt before the technician can accept. Batch C extends that path for managed IT coverage and settles completed appointments. Starting or ending a screen share leaves the request and payment state unchanged. The technician reports completion through IT Services, where customer confirmation and settlement remain associated with the case.

Rails and Gatekeeper authorize the SDP exchange against the case, roles, exact login sessions, and current customer approval. The service accepts one send-only video offer and one receive-only answer. SDP stays encrypted in the database until the session closes or expires. Browsers send encrypted WebRTC screen video directly or through TURN. Rails carries signaling and lease renewal traffic. The endpoint requires HTTPS outside loopback development.

For off-network use, configure `it_remote_support.stun_urls`, `it_remote_support.turn_urls`, and `it_remote_support.turn_secret` in Rails encrypted credentials. The TURN deployment must accept time-limited shared-secret credentials. Test TURN over TLS from the networks you support. Without TURN, connectivity depends on the networks and available ICE candidates. LiveKit is suitable for managed relaying and server-side disconnection. Its ordinary room join token is a bearer credential. This implementation uses login-bound signaling and browser-enforced teardown of the direct WebRTC connection. Revocation blocks subsequent exchanges immediately. An active browser closes the stream on its next failed authorization check. The browser also stops sending while it cannot renew its authorization lease. Changing devices or losing the WebRTC connection permanently requires a new request and fresh customer approval.

Request approval expires in five minutes. An approved session lasts up to 30 minutes. One pending or approved screen-share request per customer and per technician can be open at a time. The Rails worker clears expired signaling. Screen-sharing audit metadata is attached to the IT Service Request. It records request, consent, signaling milestones, revocation or expiry. Browser capture depends on screen-sharing support and OS permission. The picker controls the available displays and windows. This flow covers screen viewing and does not require a managed endpoint agent.

## File transfers

Set `it_remote_support.clamd_port` in Rails encrypted credentials to the TCP port of a ClamAV daemon bound to loopback on the Rails job host. Keep the port private. The daemon must support INSTREAM and accept files up to 1 MB. Run the default job queue on a host that can reach the configured loopback daemon.

Participants offer text, log, PNG, and JPEG files one at a time. Each request is limited to six files of at most 1 MB. The dashboard shows the scan state and lets the intended recipient explicitly accept each clean file. An attachment download can be claimed once and includes its SHA-256 digest. The browser saves the download using its normal download behavior. No endpoint path, clipboard, or executable deployment is exposed. A positive scan quarantines the transfer in the audit trail and discards the bytes. Scanner failures also discard the bytes and block the download. Session end cancels pending transfers and removes their encrypted payloads.

The case records who requested, accepted, cancelled, or downloaded a transfer. File names and file contents stay out of the Nevaeh receipt. The session receipt records customer consent, the technician browser's display report if present, and why the session ended. Existing work verification, customer confirmation, and Dymond Bank settlement still govern case completion and payment.
